# Part proofs and approval marks

Before a bulletin prints, the person who wrote each
part can read back just their own words, on one page,
and say "yes, that is what I wrote" — and the office
can see at a glance which parts have been read back
and which have changed since.

## What a part proof is

A part proof is a PDF of one part's own words, on
their own page, made to be read back to the person
who wrote them.

One filed file is one part is one proof. A file with
several headed blocks is one proof of the whole
file's words, not one proof per block. The proof is
made by re-reading the filed file in the Filed
folder at the moment you ask for it — the file is
the part. If that file is gone, the proof is refused
in plain words ("the filed file is no longer in the
Filed folder, so there is nothing to read back");
the proof is never guessed from the bulletin.

The proof holds exactly that part's words and
nothing else. Words marked spoken-only are left
out: a proof is a reading copy, so it shows what
will be printed, not what is only said aloud. The
heading names the church, the person the words came
from, and the Sunday, when those are known. A proof
is one page, Letter size, like the music proof.

## Which parts get a proof

Announcements, Sunday school and youth parts, and
service parts (readings, prayers) get part proofs.
They all arrive the same way and ride the same
composition.

Loose sheets (inserts) do not. An insert is its own
small document with its own print path, and its
words never ride a part proof. A file that holds
only a loose sheet has no parts to prove, and the
app says so in plain words: it "holds only a loose
sheet, and loose sheets have no part proof in this
version."

The music proof is separate and unchanged: it still
covers the music, and a part proof never carries
music.

## How to make one, and how it goes back

On the Print step, beside the "Music proof..."
button, there is a "Part proof" section listing the
filed parts for this Sunday. Each filed part has its
own "Part proof..." button. It opens a save dialog
— the file is saved with a name built from the
part's file, such as `announcements-proof.pdf` —
and the app makes the PDF the same way it makes the
music proof.

The app never sends a proof. The label on the Print
step says it plainly: "Save the file and send it
yourself, by the channel the part came by — the app
never sends it." The status line after saving says
the same: "Send it yourself, by the channel the
part came by." You send the saved file yourself, by
whatever channel the part came by — reply, attach,
or print and hand over.

## What an approval mark means

There are two approval marks, and they are the
whole scope. There is no pastor approval mark.

- **The contributor approves their words.** The
  person who wrote the part has read the proof and
  said yes, that is what they wrote.
- **Check approves the assembly.** A check of the
  bulletin came back with nothing red, with this
  part's words in place as they stand.

A mark is a note of what somebody said, with the
day they said it. It is not a claim that the words
are true, or correct, or fit to print — only that
the named person approved them as they stand, on
the named day. No proof, mark, or report ever
claims the words were checked and found true.

A mark is derived, never kept by hand. What the app
remembers is a fingerprint of the part's words at
the moment of approval, kept in the app's own
settings beside the remembered bulletins (up to 20
files, most recent last). Each time the part is
shown, the app compares the fingerprint it
remembered with the words as they stand now:

- Still the same words: the mark shows as
  approved, with its date — "Approved by the
  contributor on Sunday, November 8, 2026."
- Words changed since: the mark shows as changed,
  with the date of the approval it no longer
  matches — "Changed since the contributor
  approved it on Sunday, November 8, 2026."

Any edit to the part's words clears its mark by
itself, because the fingerprint no longer matches.
Nothing has to be un-approved by hand, so a mark
can never go stale. A part can be approved again
after an edit; the new mark dates the new words,
never the old ones.

Marks belong to one bulletin file. Next Sunday is a
new bulletin file, so it starts the new week
without the old marks.

## How each mark is recorded

Nothing is approved without a person saying so.
The app never approves a part on its own.

- **Contributor mark:** after the contributor has
  read the proof and said yes, the admin taps the
  "Record contributor approval" button beside that
  part on the Print step. The button appears only
  while the contributor mark is not live — once the
  mark is live there is nothing to record. The app
  answers: "Recorded: the contributor approved
  [file] on [day]."
- **Check mark:** recorded on its own at the same
  moment a check comes back with nothing red — the
  same moment the whole-bulletin checked mark is
  recorded. Check never approves an assembly it
  just flagged. A part with no readable words earns
  no mark: there is nothing to approve.

## Where marks show

- **In the Filed list on the Print step.** Each
  filed part shows its marks beside its file name:
  approved, or changed since approval, with the
  date either way.
- **On the proof itself.** A proof carries one line
  at the end, after the words, naming what was
  approved and when: "The contributor approved
  these words on [day]." If the contributor has
  not approved it, a live Check mark names the
  assembly instead: "Check approved this assembly
  on [day]." A part with no live mark carries no
  line, and a changed-since mark earns no line
  either: a proof must never claim words that have
  since changed. The line is not one of the part's
  words.
- **On the Check step.** Check gains one summary
  note beside its existing assembly checks, in the
  form "Part approvals: 3 parts approved, 1
  changed since approval". It is silent when no
  filed part carries any approval record at all. A
  part counts as approved when either kind's mark
  is live; it counts as changed when a mark was
  recorded but neither kind is live.

The whole-bulletin checked mark behaves exactly as
before; a part mark changes nothing about it.

## What a mark does not do

- A mark never gates printing. Printing proceeds
  with or without any mark, the way a missing
  license number never blocks printing.
- A mark is not a check of the words. The
  contributor mark says the contributor recognised
  their own words; the Check mark says a clean
  check passed with those words in place. Neither
  says the words are right.
- A proof shows what the bulletin shows. If a
  serving line comes out empty where names belong,
  that is the known rename-cycle blind spot
  showing — two renames swapping exactly each
  other's places leave the name lookup with
  nowhere safe to go, so it shows nothing rather
  than somebody else's names — not a fault in the
  proof. Its cure is row identity, which belongs
  to the editor work, not to proofs.
